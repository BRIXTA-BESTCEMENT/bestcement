02  Timeline

How it unfolded

Key events as recorded in the order.

  1. 4 Jul 2005

    Company incorporated

    J.U.D. Cements Limited is incorporated in Meghalaya.

  2. 30 Dec 2024

    Debenture Trust Deed signed

    Catalyst Trusteeship Limited, appointed Debenture Trustee in November 2024, executes the Trust Deed and security documents with the company.

  3. 2 Jan 2025

    ₹155 crore raised through NCDs

    The money is credited to an escrow account in the company's name, operated on the trustee's written instructions.

  4. 31 Jan 2025

    First coupon goes unpaid

    The Tribunal treats this as the date of default for the coupon.

  5. Aug – Oct 2025

    Defaults acknowledged

    A letter of 21 August 2025 and a Deed of Undertaking of 28 October 2025 acknowledge the payment defaults.

  6. Oct 2025 – Jan 2026

    ₹5.64 crore appropriated

    Sums are taken from the escrow account towards the liability in four tranches.

  7. 5 Mar 2026

    Recall notice issued

    The trustee demands the outstanding principal and dues within seven days. The period ends on 12 March 2026, the date of default for the principal.

  8. May 2026

    Petition filed

    After the debenture holders authorise the trustee on 14 May 2026, the Section 7 petition is filed before the NCLT, Guwahati Bench.

  9. 17 Sep 2026

    NCLAT allows a reply

    The company is permitted to file its reply subject to costs, and the Tribunal is asked to take the matter to its logical end in accordance with law.

  10. 12 Oct 2026

    Petition admitted

    A request to defer the pronouncement is rejected. The petition is admitted, CIRP begins and an Interim Resolution Professional is appointed.

03  Findings

What the Tribunal decided

Nine questions were framed. Tap one to see the company's objection and the Tribunal's answer.

Question 1Can the Debenture Trustee file without joining the debenture holders?Yes, trustee has standing

Company's objection

The trustee only represents the holders, and the holders' own conduct was central to the defence, so they had to be made parties.

Tribunal's answer

The trustee acts under the Trust Deed and written authorisations of 14 May 2026. No relief is sought against the holders, so their presence was not essential.

Question 2Is the ₹155 crore a financial debt under Section 5(8)?Yes

Company's objection

The money went into an escrow account the company could not operate, so it was never really disbursed to the company.

Tribunal's answer

Debentures are expressly covered by Section 5(8)(c). The funds reached an account in the company's own name and were earmarked for its purposes. Limits on how money is used do not cancel the borrowing.

Question 3Was there a default, and does the escrow arrangement excuse it?Default established

Company's objection

Enough money sat in the escrow account and fixed deposits controlled by the trustee, which could have paid the coupon and did not.

Tribunal's answer

The Trust Deed puts the duty to pay on the company. The trustee's power to use escrow funds is a remedy after default, not a condition for payment. No refused request to release funds for the coupon was shown.

Question 4What weight do the August and October 2025 acknowledgements carry?They support the default

Company's objection

They were signed under commercial pressure while operations were stalled, to get the company's own money released.

Tribunal's answer

No coercion or duress was pleaded and the documents were never challenged. They confirm the liability and the default.

Question 5Does the date of default affect whether the petition can proceed?Partly accepted, petition stands

Company's objection

31 January 2025 cannot be the date of default for the whole principal.

Tribunal's answer

Agreed on that point. The coupon defaulted on 31 January 2025 and the principal on 12 March 2026. The coupon default alone crosses the legal threshold.

Question 6Is the information utility's record of default enough?Default proved by other records

Company's objection

The authentication of the NeSL record and the service of notice on the company were disputed.

Tribunal's answer

The record shows as deemed authenticated. It is in any case not the only proof. Default was established from the contracts, acknowledgements and pleadings.

Question 7Do errors in the claim calculation stop admission?To be reconciled, not a bar

Company's objection

Figures differ by ₹5.83 crore, a premium was included before it was due, and no credit was given for ₹5.64 crore appropriated or ₹2.76 crore of deposit interest.

Tribunal's answer

These need reconciliation and verification, but they do not defeat admission once the debt and a default above the threshold are proved.

Question 8Was the petition a recovery tactic under Section 65?No case made out

Company's objection

The petition was coercive, with the recall timed to the month production started.

Tribunal's answer

These are contractual disputes, and nothing shows the petition was filed fraudulently or maliciously. The company may still apply separately under Section 65.

Question 9Does a solvent, operating company with strong assets avoid admission?No

Company's objection

The plant is running, assets are valued at about ₹801 crore, and new offtake demand was expected.

Tribunal's answer

Under the amended Section 7(5), admission turns on debt and default, not on solvency. Asset value and prospects may matter within the resolution process itself.

04  Directions

What the order directs

The operative directions of the Tribunal, in plain language.

1

CIRP begins

The petition is admitted and the insolvency resolution process runs from 12 October 2026.

2

IRP appointed

Mr. Avishek Gupta is appointed Interim Resolution Professional. His authorisation is valid till 31 December 2026.

3

Public announcement

The IRP must publish the public announcement within three days of the order, inviting claims.

4

Management moves to the IRP

The IRP runs the company. Staff and promoters are legally bound to cooperate, and the IRP can approach the Tribunal if they do not.

5

Assets and records

The IRP takes charge of the company's assets and documents without delay, with police assistance if needed.

6

Reports and notices

The IRP reports progress to the Tribunal and serves the order on tax, GST and provident fund authorities and on employee associations.

The moratorium protects the company from

ANew or continuing lawsuits and proceedings

BTransfer or sale of its assets

CEnforcement of security, including under SARFAESI

DRecovery of property it occupies by an owner or lessor

It lasts until the resolution process ends, a resolution plan is approved, or liquidation is ordered.
